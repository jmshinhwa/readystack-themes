Questions

What does it actually do?

It reads a config file line by line and reports the spellings that parse but do not mean what you wrote - status = "ok", a compatible with no comma, address-cells without the #, an unquoted TOML edition. 23 rules over seven languages, plus 50 snippets. No toolchain, no build context, no network.

Do the free devicetree extensions not already do this?

They highlight and complete, and inside a configured Zephyr or nRF context they resolve bindings - that part they do better than this pack. They do not grade a bare .overlay pasted out of a vendor forum, and they say nothing about the TOML, .properties, .ahk, .rpy or .ink files sitting in the same repo.

Why does an AI assistant get this wrong?

Because enabled is the English word and every other config format in the repo uses it. Devicetree does not: the value that turns a node on is okay and nothing else. It is the exact shape of mistake that reads correct and builds clean.
